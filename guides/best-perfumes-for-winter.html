The best perfumes for winter: warm, cosy scents that last

Winter calls for richer perfumes. Warm notes like amber, vanilla, oud and spice hold up in cold air and feel right with heavy coats and scarves.

By Saffron & Still, Cheltenham · Updated November 2026

What makes a good winter perfume?

Cold air slows down how a perfume spreads, so light citrus and green notes can disappear quickly. Heavier ingredients last longer and feel warmer on the skin. Look for these notes when you shop:

  • Vanilla, tonka and praline for a soft, sweet warmth
  • Amber, labdanum and resins for a glowing, cosy trail
  • Oud, saffron and leather for depth and evening wear
  • Coffee, cacao and spice for a rich, edible feel

How to make your perfume last in the cold

  • Spray onto moisturised skin. Fragrance holds on better to skin that isn't dry.
  • Apply to your neck, wrists and the inside of your elbows before you get dressed.
  • A light spray on a scarf keeps the scent around you for longer. Test on a hidden spot first, as some perfumes can mark delicate fabric.
  • Choose an extrait de parfum if you want the longest wear. It has a higher concentration, so you need fewer sprays.

Common questions

Why does my summer perfume fade so fast in winter?
Cold air slows how quickly a perfume evaporates, so light, airy notes can smell faint. Warmer, heavier notes such as amber, vanilla, oud and resins carry better in the cold.
Can I still wear fresh perfume in winter?
Yes. Fresh scents work well for the office or the gym. If yours feels weak, look for one with a woody or musky base, which lasts longer on cold days.
How many sprays should I use?
Two to four sprays of an eau de parfum is plenty for most people. Extraits de parfum are stronger, so one to three sprays is usually enough.
