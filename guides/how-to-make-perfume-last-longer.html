How to make perfume last longer

Why perfume fades, ten simple tips, and six long-lasting picks.

How to make perfume last longer

By Saffron & Still, Cheltenham · Perfume guide · November 2026

You spray your favourite perfume in the morning, and by lunch it seems to have gone. It is one of the questions we get asked most in Cheltenham. The good news is that a few small changes make a big difference.

Why does perfume fade?

Every perfume is built in three layers. The top notes are what you smell first: citrus, fruit, light herbs. They are small, light molecules that evaporate within minutes. The heart notes come through next, usually florals and spices, and last a few hours. The base notes are the heavy, slow ones: woods, musk, amber, vanilla, resins, leather and oud. They are what you still smell at the end of the day.

So a perfume that is mostly bright citrus will always fade faster than one built on amber and woods. Your skin plays a part too. Dry skin holds scent less well than moisturised skin, and warm skin pushes perfume out faster.

Top notesMinutes. Citrus, fruit, light herbs
Heart notesA few hours. Florals and spices
Base notesAll day. Woods, musk, amber, vanilla, resins

10 simple ways to make perfume last longer

  1. Spray after a shower. Clean, slightly warm skin with open pores takes perfume in well. Pat yourself dry first.
  2. Moisturise first. Perfume clings to moisturised skin much better than dry skin. Use an unscented cream or body oil so it does not clash.
  3. Aim for pulse points. Inner wrists, the sides of the neck, behind the ears and the inner elbows. These spots are warm and help the scent move gently through the day.
  4. Don't rub your wrists together. It warms and spreads the perfume too quickly, so the top notes disappear faster. Spray, then leave it to dry.
  5. Spray your clothes too. Fabric holds scent far longer than skin. A light mist on a scarf, jumper or jacket collar can last for days. Test a hidden spot first on delicate fabrics.
  6. Mist your hair, not soak it. Hair holds scent well. Spray your hairbrush and brush it through, rather than spraying straight onto your hair.
  7. Choose a stronger concentration. An extrait de parfum has more perfume oil than an eau de parfum, and an eau de parfum has more than an eau de toilette. Higher concentration usually means longer wear.
  8. Pick perfumes with a rich base. Look for musk, amber, vanilla, woods, leather or oud in the base notes. Every product page in our shop lists the top, heart and base notes, so you can check before you buy.
  9. Store it properly. Heat, light and steam break perfume down. Keep bottles in their box or a drawer, not on a sunny windowsill or in the bathroom.
  10. Carry a top-up. For a long day or an evening out, a small travel spray means you can refresh in seconds.
Can't smell it any more? It may not have faded at all. Your nose gets used to a smell quickly and stops noticing it. Before you spray again, ask a friend, or step outside for a minute and try again.

How many sprays should you use?

For most perfumes, two to four sprays is plenty. With an extrait, start with one or two, because the concentration is higher and it carries further than you think. If you are wearing it to the office or somewhere close-up, go lighter. For an evening out, add a spray on your clothes rather than piling more onto your skin.

What about layering?

Layering simply means wearing two scents together, or the same scent in two forms. The easiest trick is to spray a long-lasting, musky or woody perfume first, then a fresher one on top. The base keeps going after the fresh notes fade, so the whole thing lasts longer. Start with one spray of each and see how they sit together.

Common questions

Why can't I smell my perfume after an hour?
Often it is still there. Your nose gets used to a smell quickly and stops noticing it. Ask a friend, or smell your wrist after stepping outside for a minute.
Does rubbing your wrists together make perfume fade?
It can. Rubbing warms and spreads the perfume quickly, so the lighter top notes burn off faster. Spray and let it dry on its own.
Is an extrait de parfum stronger than an eau de parfum?
Yes. An extrait has a higher concentration of perfume oil, so it usually lasts longer and smells richer, with a closer trail. You need fewer sprays.
Where is the best place to keep perfume?
Somewhere cool, dark and dry, in its box or a drawer. Avoid sunny windowsills and steamy bathrooms, as heat and light break perfume down.
Is it safe to spray perfume on clothes?
On most fabrics, yes, and scent lasts longer on fabric than skin. Test a hidden spot first on silk and pale or delicate fabrics, as some perfumes can mark them.
